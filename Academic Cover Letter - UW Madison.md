Dear Members of the Search Committee,

I am writing to apply for the Assistant Professor of Music Technology and Artificial Intelligence position (RISE-AI) at the Mead Witter School of Music.

Three years ago, I discovered a deep curiosity with AI and the unknown, seemingly unlimited, possibilities. I began exploring how this incredible technology could help me as an independent musician and composer. After two years working in prompt engineering and data annotation for AI music analysis and synthesis, I found the fear of AI disrupting creative industries to be rooted more in science fiction than reality.

This fascination led to The Composer Compass, an AI composer assistant. This tool does not compose music; it learns your compositional process and augments your creative capacity through a structured protocol in the background and a simple interface in the foreground.

The composer develops an aesthetic blueprint and interactively sets an identity and goal for the piece, then composes. The composer shares updates with the tool via text and XML files, which allows for real musical analysis, not generic chatbot discussion. Through a set of Model Context Protocol connections, a tailored retrieval-augmented generation pipeline, and a multi-dimensional rubric, the tool helps a composer navigate the life cycle of making music they're proud of.

The Composer Compass can also be used to teach someone how to compose. As with all AI, it cannot think for you. Rather, it develops your own creative mind and process in real time. Having used it for my last three pieces, I can attest to its utility and educational possibilities.

In conjunction with the production of this tool, and others, I have documented the entire process and have distilled it into a curriculum and AI teaching philosophy for novice AI users. It identifies necessary non-musical knowledge, the common pitfalls of AI development, and the framework to develop any AI tool that the student may find useful and inspiring.

As excited as I am about the future of AI, I cannot ignore the real sustainability and ethical issues that it poses. While this is still challenging to reconcile, AI is not going away. It is imperative we educate this generation on the great responsibility that comes with the power we all have at our fingertips. As a society we are still figuring this out, and my favorite part about teaching is that it is the greatest way to learn.

My goal is to have a measurable and positive impact on the future of AI. As a UW alum, I am inspired by, and proud of, the future goals of the RISE-AI program. I would be honored to be at the forefront of creating a landscape where AI will be seen as one of humanity's greatest achievements, not a catastrophic missed opportunity.

I am happy to share my AI portfolio as well as my curriculum drafts. I look forward to discussing my ideas for the future of AI at the University of Wisconsin.

Thank you for your time and consideration.
