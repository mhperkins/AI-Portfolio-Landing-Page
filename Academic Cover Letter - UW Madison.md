Dear Wendy Johnson and Search Committee,

Three years ago, I discovered a deep curiosity about AI and its seemingly unlimited possibilities. I began exploring how it could help me as an independent musician and composer. After two years in prompt engineering and data annotation for AI music analysis and generation, I found the fear of AI disrupting creative industries rooted more in science fiction than reality.

This fascination led to The Composer Compass, an AI composer assistant. It does not compose music; it learns your compositional process and augments your creative capacity through a structured protocol in the background and a simple interface in the foreground.

The composer develops an aesthetic blueprint and interactively sets an identity and goal for the piece. The composer shares updates via text and XML files, enabling real musical analysis rather than generic chatbot discussions. Through Model Context Protocol, a tailored retrieval-augmented generation pipeline, and a multi-dimensional rubric, the tool helps a composer navigate the life cycle of making music they're proud of.

The Composer Compass can also teach someone to compose. Like all AI, it cannot think for you; it develops your own creative mind and process in real time. Having used it for my last three pieces, I can attest to its utility and educational value.

Alongside building this tool and others, I have documented the entire process and distilled it into a curriculum and AI teaching philosophy for novice users. It identifies the non-musical knowledge required, common pitfalls of AI development, and a framework for developing any AI tool a student finds useful and inspiring.

As excited as I am about AI's future, I cannot ignore the real sustainability and ethical issues it poses. These remain hard to reconcile, but AI is not going away. It is imperative we educate this generation on the great responsibility that comes with the power at our fingertips. As a society we are still learning this, and my favorite part of teaching is that it's the greatest way to learn.

My goal is to educate students while researching and developing new ways for AI to responsibly integrate into music and dispel reasonable fears. As a UW alum, I'm inspired by and proud of RISE-AI's goals. I would be honored to help build a landscape where AI is remembered as one of humanity's greatest achievements, not a catastrophic missed opportunity.

I am happy to share my AI portfolio and curriculum drafts and look forward to discussing my ideas for the future of AI at the University of Wisconsin - Madison.

Thank you for your time and consideration,
