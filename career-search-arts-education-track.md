## The Overqualification Problem

The gap: Max ran the strategy, finances, sales, and every operational hat at Sprout Society. The
target role is an hourly, non-exempt associate seat with a manager above it, focused on day-to-day
program delivery and front-line family communication. Read at full altitude, the resume signals a
director candidate applying for a coordinator job, which reads as flight risk, not as strength.

**The fixes:**

1. **Use the real title.** "Community Manager," not "Operations Manager." It is accurate and it
   already sits closer to the target role's register.
2. **Control altitude in the language.** Drop "strategic," "business analysis," "revenue model,"
   "sales," and "founder" framing from the lead bullets. Replace with "coordinate," "maintain,"
   "respond to," "support," "assist." Same facts, lower register.
3. **Keep the membership win, shrink it, move it down.** It is real proof of initiative, but it is
   the most C-suite-sounding line on the resume. One line, near the bottom, not the headline.
4. **Sequence bullets by the job posting's own priority order**, not by what feels most impressive.
   WCO lists program operations and family communication first at 90% of the role; community
   events and partnerships second at 10%. Mirror that order.
5. **Soft-pedal the internal AI tools.** Per the hard rule in `career-search-ops-track.md`, default
   to omitting "AI" branding for traditional arts/nonprofit roles. Translate the outcome into plain
   language: kept records accurate, cut manual data entry, retired subscriptions no longer needed.
   Use it only to back up a stated JD need (here: "proficiency in database or CRM tools; comfort
   learning new systems quickly"), not as a headline achievement.
6. **Cut signals of upward career-track ambition from this version.** PMP-in-progress and "Founder"
   both signal a candidate aiming at a director track, which raises the same flight-risk flag.
   Omit or minimize for this track.
7. **Never explain the overqualification in the letter.** Do not write "I know this is a step
   down" or anything adjacent. Volunteering the caveat is what draws attention to it. Let the
   resume's altitude do the work silently.
8. **If asked directly in an interview**, answer with the real, positive reason: hands-on
   community and education work, hours that fit, mission fit with arts access. Never "this is
   temporary" or "I'm overqualified but."
