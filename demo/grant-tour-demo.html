✓ Brief linked 🔗 Open Google Doc → Last updated Aug 27, 2026
FUNDER PROFILE Brooklyn Neighbors Fund is a community foundation that gives small grants to resident-led groups across the borough. The problem it is trying to solve: neighbors who live side by side for years and never meet.
FIT ANALYSIS Strengths: free to join, peer-led, rooted in one neighborhood. Gap: outcomes beyond attendance. Show who comes back, not only who comes. Eligibility risk: annual expenses must be under $250K. Confirm before writing. Verdict: GO
GRANTEE RESEARCH 5 comparable past grantees, from public tax filings: Maple Street Tenants Circle, Eastside Garden Keepers, Knit & Natter Collective, Corner Table Supper Club, Block by Block Chess Club. Patterns: volunteer-run, budgets under $150K, the neighborhood named in the first sentence.
VOICE & LANGUAGE Use: "neighbors supporting neighbors", "resident-led", "belonging", "participatory". Swap: "clients" → "members", "services" → "gatherings", "therapy" → "peer support".
APPLICATION INTELLIGENCE 9 questions, each with a 1,500 character limit. Residents on the advisory council score every application. Decisions in December.
PROGRAM OFFICER OUTREACH One question only: does peer connection fit Civic Life or Health and Wellbeing?

Describe your organization and the neighborhood it serves.
💡 Lead with belonging and name the neighborhood in the first sentence.
✏ Edit🗑
Sprout Society is a free, peer-led community space in Brooklyn, run by neighbors for neighbors who want more connection in their lives. Every week the doors open for Co-Working on Tuesdays and peer-led gatherings in the evenings, and a few times a year for Sprout N Tell, a night where local artists and musicians share their work with a room full of people who live nearby. Nobody pays to come in, nobody needs a referral, and nobody has to explain why they showed up. Most people arrive knowing no one and leave with a name, a reason to come back, and often a new friend who lives on the next block. Members lead most of what happens here: they host the gatherings, set up the chairs, greet people at the door, and decide what the space should try next. Over the summer, members hosted their own gatherings every week, from a Sunday walking group to a zine table. We started because so many neighbors told us the same thing, that they had lived in Brooklyn for years and still felt alone in it. A third space that asks nothing of you turned out to be what people were missing. This grant would keep the doors open more nights a week and help more of our neighbors find their way in.
